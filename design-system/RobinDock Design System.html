01 — Foundations

Two registers, one system

RobinDock serves two people from one brand: the owner / office manager who must trust it with patient records, and the front-desk or tech staffer who lives in it all shift. The brand runs in two modes built from the same palette, type, and shapes — so it always reads as one product. Only the intensity changes, never the ingredients.

Mode A — default
Calm
Light, airy, legible

Pale gradient washes, white surfaces, hairlines, big quiet headlines, generous space. Apple-clean — nothing competes with the content.

Use for: the entire product UI, most of the marketing page, anything read or worked in for long stretches. ~80% of every surface.

Mode B — accent
Confident ·
Bold color blocks, platform presence

Deep indigo or royal blocks, the "three clouds" framing, bold stat bands. Platform confidence — used sparingly, for impact.

Use for: the Flock / network story, pricing, marketing hero accents, dashboards. A deliberate accent — never the document-triage screens.

The rule: calm is the default and carries roughly 80% of every surface. Confident mode is a deliberate accent — a few moments per marketing page, and in-product only on dashboards or the Flock view. What keeps it one brand: identical palette, Inter type, pill / card shapes, and the robin mark across both modes.

03 — Foundations

The three clouds

RobinDock's platform is framed as three "clouds," and each owns one accent color. This is the bridge between the two modes: on marketing it's bold cloud cards; in-product it's the same colors as quiet tags, top-bars, and dots. One mental model, head to tail.

Document Cloud

Capture & organize every fax, lab, and referral.
Royal blue. Primary actions, document tiles, "tag to pet."

Patient Cloud

Tag documents to the right patient & timeline.
Violet. Patient tags, secondary actions, referral type.

Flock Network

Exchange documents directly between practices.
Aqua. "Via Flock" tags, network status, connected nodes.
Why it works: a referral that arrives via the Flock gets an aqua tag; a patient timeline accent is violet; a primary "Tag to pet" button is royal. Users learn the color language without being taught it — and the marketing site's bold cloud cards reinforce the exact same code.

37 — Patterns

Registers in context

The same content, placed in each register. Calm is the working surface — the document is the hero. Confident is a deliberate accent for the platform / network story. The rule of thumb: if a user reads or works here for minutes at a time, keep it calm.

Calm · default · ~80%
Calm, secure,
in order.

Product UI, list and triage screens, long-form reading, most marketing.

Confident · accent · ≤20%
Your practice,
connected.

The Flock, pricing, dashboards, hero accents, the bulk-action bar.

Both surfaces share the exact same palette, Inter type, pill / card shapes, and robin mark. Only the intensity changes — that's what keeps it one brand across the marketing site and the app.
38 — Patterns

Voice

Plain, calm, and reassuring — the way a trusted colleague talks. Short sentences. Active verbs from the product's own vocabulary: capture, tag, route, store. Confidence comes from clarity, never hype.

Sounds like us
  • "Calm, secure, in order."
  • "3 need routing"
  • "All caught up"
  • "Tag to a pet"
  • "No fax round-trip."
Not us
  • "Revolutionary AI-powered synergy"
  • "ERROR: 3 items require action!"
  • "You have no documents."
  • "Associate this file with a patient record"
  • Exclamation marks & jargon
Use the workflow verbs consistently — capture → tag → route → store — across UI labels, empty states, and marketing. Status reads as a calm fact ("Routed," "Needs review"), never an alarm.
39 — Patterns

Do & don't

The rules that keep the system coherent as it grows.

Do
  • Keep the product in calm mode — light surfaces, the document is the hero.
  • Reserve indigo / royal blocks for the Flock, pricing, dashboards, and marketing accents.
  • Tie each accent to its meaning: royal = action, violet = patient, aqua = network.
  • Let headlines carry weight through scale, not decoration.
  • Use generous whitespace and hairlines to keep dense screens calm.
  • Keep one royal primary action per view.
Don't
  • Drop dark indigo blocks into document-triage or list screens.
  • Use violet and aqua interchangeably — they carry different meaning.
  • Stack multiple bold color blocks back-to-back; one accent moment at a time.
  • Add a second typeface or decorative display font.
  • Let confident mode exceed ~20% of any surface.
  • Invent new accent colors — the four jobs are fixed.
When in doubt, choose calm. The product earns trust by getting out of the way; the brand earns confidence in a few well-chosen moments.
